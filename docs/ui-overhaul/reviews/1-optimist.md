# Optimist: what to protect

## Headline
Under a crowded, drum-machine skin, Proof has unusually good substance. The recipe page is built around what a baker actually needs. Baking mode is a real kitchen tool: one big instruction, a phase row, Back/Next at thumb height. The gallery's "one row per recipe, oldest bake on the left" shows improvement, which is the product's whole idea. The copy is honest and kind throughout ("No account. Nothing to set up.", "Your own cookbook works offline"). The overhaul should change the clothes and keep the skeleton. Most of what I would protect is information architecture, behaviour and voice, not the LED aesthetic. The things I would carry across are the phase-based method, the iteration rows, the honest empty states and the photo-first cookbook grid.

## Must survive the overhaul
Ranked.
1. **The bake log as a peer of the recipe.** Keep the `Previous makes (N)` tab, the per-recipe history rows and "Lab notes & iterations". Never demote them.
2. **Gallery rows: one recipe per row, oldest to newest, numbered, dated.** This shows improvement, which is the point of the app.
3. **Baking mode's single-instruction focus**, with a huge step, `STEP n / N`, and Back/Next at the bottom of the phone.
4. **Phase grouping of the method** (COOK / FINISH headings) with absolute step numbers shared between the recipe page and baking mode.
5. **Hands-free kit**: voice, wave, swipe, timers parsed from step text, wake lock, smart ingredient highlighting.
6. **Ingredients beside the method on desktop** with aligned quantities, scale 0.5x–3x, baker's %, copy list, and the pantry check plus `SUB`.
7. **Photo-first cookbook grid** with folder/chapter counts, search and shuffle.
8. **Personal Bests and Recently Baked shelves** on the home screen.
9. **Honest data presentation**: the app shows `--` instead of a fake 50 minutes, draws no fake charts, and says when there is not enough data.
10. **The shared recipe page**: same layout, `Shared by @handle`, one primary action, `Save to my cookbook`.
11. **Starter shelf for an empty cookbook** and "Or start from a blank page."
12. **Local-first honesty copy**: "No account. Nothing to set up.", "Only on this device", "Your own cookbook works offline", and Export .zip as the free backup.
13. **Pantry to Grocery list**: stock check, then "what do I need to buy", then Share.
14. **Light and dark themes**, with light's warm paper tone.
15. **44px touch targets** and the four-slot phone dock with a floating `+`.

## Ideas worth amplifying
1. **Recipe as a printed page.** Keep the two-column layout (ingredients left, method right), but set it as a cookbook spread. Use a serif title, a hairline rule under "Ingredients" and "Method", and drop caps or a numbered-step style. The structure already exists in regular-02-recipe__light__w1280__page.png.
2. **Bake history as a contact sheet or "proof strip".** The gallery's numbered, dated rows suit a film-strip look. Add a short handwritten-style note under each frame ("a little too brown", "best crumb"). Mark the personal best with a ribbon.
3. **The phase row becomes a chapter timeline.** Keep the colour-coded running/next/done logic, but render it as a ribbon bookmark or a progress line of named stages (Mix · Bulk · Shape · Proof · Bake) in the recipe header and baking mode. The idea of "where am I" stays.
4. **Cookbook home as a table of contents.** The folder rail with counts already reads like chapters ("Dessert 44, Side 31"). Present them as chapter tabs or an index page, with Personal Bests as "Favourites in your hand".
5. **Light theme as the hero.** The warm bone paper in light mode can lead the brand. Dark should become a warm "evening kitchen" dark rather than pure black, with the same ink hierarchy.
6. **Share as a first-class page.** The `/r/:slug` page is already complete and has one action. Add a printed recipe-card preview, a QR and a short "from @handle's kitchen" credit. Make a baker's profile read like an "about the author" page.
7. **Notes in the margin.** Show the last bake's note ("less sugar, 5 min longer") beside the relevant step in the method, so the log informs the recipe. It is the app's best differentiator and fits a cookbook's marginalia tradition.
8. **Keep baking mode dark and large.** A kitchen at dawn is its own context. Keep the single big instruction, the dark option and the huge Next key, but soften the monospace to a readable text face.
9. **Make empty states charming and useful.** The starter shelf ("Start from one of these") and the honest "not enough data" messages are great. Carry the voice through the new design with warmer illustration or photography.

### Changed or added under-sold items
- Checkable ingredients and in-step timer chips (A1, A2) are daily-use features with no visual emphasis at all. A cookbook design should print the chips like a margin note ("⏱ 60 min") and give the ticks a pencil-mark feel.
- "FROM OTHER BAKERS" and the Lab Notes panel (A3, A4) already offer the sentence-level voice a cookbook wants. Keep the titles, change the frame.

### Changed ideas
- Idea 7 (notes in the margin) is stronger now: the Lab Notes panel already sits beneath the ingredients, so the last bake's note can sit there with a "Last time:" label at no layout cost.
- Dashboard (A6): the missing-photo squares make the case for a designed fallback plate (title set in type on a colour block, like a cookbook chapter divider). This is also worth protecting as a rule: no tile ever shows a bare empty box.
